Overview

Rising demand for electric vehicle batteries is driving the need for efficient lithium refining. This site explores the MVR (Mechanical Vapor Recompression) evaporative crystallizer, a key technology for producing battery-grade Lithium Hydroxide Monohydrate (LiOH·H2O).

What this site does

This interactive tool compares single-effect, multi-effect, and MVR evaporation strategies. It teaches the underlying physics—from solubility and Boiling Point Elevation (BPE) to compressor thermodynamics—and reveals why MVR is often the most energy-efficient choice despite using more expensive electricity.

What I learned

  • The critical role of BPE in determining the "lift" required by an MVR compressor.
  • How electricity-vs-steam price crossovers dictate the economic choice of technology.
  • The trade-off between crystal yield and purity controlled by the purge ratio.

Industry Context

Tesla Robstown Refinery

The Tesla lithium refinery in Robstown, TX (operational as of Jan 2026) aims to produce about 50 GWh/year of battery capacity. This requires translating spodumene concentrate through a kiln, alkaline leach, and purification steps into final crystallization.

Capacity Math: 50 GWh/yr × [Intensity kg LCE/kWh] × 1.136 t Hydrate/t LCE ≈ 40,000 t LiOH·H2O/yr ≈ 5 t/h.

Operating Problems

Foaming: LiOH solutions can foam, leading to carry-over into condensate or wet compressor suction (erosion).

Scaling: Li2CO3 scaling reduces heat transfer (U) and requires periodic washing.

Market Share

MVR technology holds approximately 55-60% of the market for new lithium crystallization plants due to its low carbon footprint and operating cost advantages in regions with stable power grids.

Theory & Terminology

1. Evaporation & BPE

Dissolved ions lower water's vapor pressure, so the liquor must be hotter to boil at the same pressure.

2. MVR as a Heat Pump

By paying for a small pressure "lift", MVR recycles the massive latent heat of the vapor.

3. Solubility & Supersaturation

Crystallization occurs when a solution is driven into the metastable zone (supersaturated). For LiOH, the solubility is relatively flat with temperature, making evaporative crystallization necessary.

Metastable Zone (Schematic):
  • Binodal: The equilibrium solubility line.
  • Metastable Limit: Growth on seeds, but no new nuclei.
  • Spinodal: Spontaneous phase separation.
