# Project brief for a UX/UI pass (for ChatGPT)

You are helping **Andrew**, a 4th-year chemical engineering student at the University of Cincinnati, improve the **UI and UX** of a finished, working, tested static website. The physics and data are done and verified. Your job is how it **looks, reads and feels**, without breaking anything listed under "Hard constraints". Work in small, reviewable change sets.

## 1. What the site is
**MVR Crystallizer Explorer**: an interactive teaching site for CHE 4061 Separation Processes (Prof. Carlos Co, Fall 2026). It explains why lithium refineries (e.g., Tesla's Robstown, TX plant) crystallize battery-grade lithium hydroxide monohydrate (LiOH·H₂O) in evaporative crystallizers driven by **mechanical vapor recompression (MVR)**, and lets you compare MVR with 1/3/5-effect steam evaporators on the same feed.

It is also an **AI project**: the plan was written by Claude, critiqued by Gemini, and built twice (Claude → site root, Gemini → `/gemini/`) so the models can be compared.

- **Live:** https://ucseparations.netlify.app (Claude build at `/`, Gemini build at `/gemini/`)
- **Repo (public):** https://github.com/be-aa8/seperations (site in `site/`)
- **Audience:** the professor and classmates. It is presented **live on Mon Dec 7, 2026** on a laptop/projector, and people will open it on phones. The professor's standard question is "how does that make sense?", so clarity of equations, units and interpretations matters more than decoration.

## 2. Structure: 7 hash-routed tabs, one page (`site/index.html`)
1. **Overview**: hook, three cards, "What I learned" (placeholder for Andrew).
2. **Industry Context**: LiOH·H₂O demand, Tesla Robstown, production-volume arithmetic, operating problems (foaming, Li₂CO₃ scaling).
3. **Theory**: about 33 KaTeX equations in groups, each with units and a one-line interpretation; glossary.
4. **Simulator**: about 22 sliders and presets (left column) → live process-flow diagram (clickable units open an equation panel) → about 35 result cards (equation + value + units + interpretation, with a "?" to expand the worked substitution) → 12 Plotly charts (P1–P11, P1b).
5. **AI Workflow**: sub-tabs "Model Comparison" (tables, findings, screenshots) and "Process Log" (placeholder).
6. **Video & Transcript**: placeholders until the YouTube upload (Dec 4).
7. **References**: generated from `data.js` (every number has a source or an ESTIMATE flag).

## 4. Hard constraints (automated tests run on every push; they must keep passing)

- **Every visible `input[type=range]`** must change a chart's data or the visible text when moved. Don't add decorative range inputs.
- No visible text `NaN`, `undefined` or `Infinity`.
- No `.katex-error`, and the Theory tab must show KaTeX.
- Every visible `.js-plotly-plot` must render (`.main-svg`).
- **Zero console errors.**
- **No horizontal page scroll at 375 px** on any tab. (Wide things such as tables and the PFD must scroll inside their own container.)

**Phase 2 browser test** (`tests/browser-phase2.mjs`): `#p9` and `#p10` render and respond to `#in-U`, `#in-loadFraction` and `#in-interest`; **at least 5** `#pfd [data-unit]` elements are clickable (and keyboard Enter works); `#pfd-panel` then shows KaTeX and no NaN.

## 5. Content rules from the course (don't weaken these in the design)
- Every calculation shown keeps its **equation (KaTeX), units, and a one-line physical interpretation** visible. You may restyle, group, or make the "?" detail nicer, but the equation must stay visible on each card.
- "No Plot = No Pay": every calculation has a plot; keep all 12 charts.
- Labels **ESTIMATE**, **SCHEMATIC** and **SOURCE NEEDED** must stay visible wherever they appear. Don't add any number that isn't already on the site or in `data.js`.
- Keep the math at a 3rd-year ChemE level; no new technical content without Andrew.
- Accessibility: keyboard navigation, visible focus, alt text, sufficient contrast, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected.

## 6. Current state: known UX problems (start here)
1. **Simulator is one very long page** (sliders, diagram, ~35 cards, 12 charts). It's hard to see cause → effect: moving a slider in the left column, the affected chart is far below. Ideas: sticky slider column on desktop; a compact "key results" strip (BPE, kWh/t, $/t, yield, optimum ΔT) near the top; collapsible card groups; group charts by question (Energy · BPE · Economics · Purity · Crystallization) with in-page jump links.
2. **Mobile**: on phones the 22 sliders come before any result; the comparison and critique tables get very narrow; the slider column could become a collapsible "Inputs" drawer or accordion.
3. **Visual hierarchy**: the header and tabs are plain; the cards are dense; no clear typographic scale; equations, values and interpretations compete. A cleaner card design (value prominent, equation below, interpretation muted) would help.
4. **Overview** is text-heavy; it could use a stronger hero with the 3–4 headline numbers from the hand calc (e.g., 29 kWh/t, COP ≈ 24, 89.6 % Li yield, optimum heater ΔT ≈ 3 K) and the process diagram as a visual.
5. **Theory** is a long list of equations; consider a sticky table of contents by group.
6. **Diagram**: some labels crowd (e.g., "circulating liquor" touches the crystallizer); units could look more like real equipment symbols. Keep it a simple inline SVG.
7. **Placeholders** (What I learned, Process Log, Video, Transcript) should look intentional ("Coming Dec 4") rather than dashed boxes.
8. **Presenting on a projector**: check contrast and font sizes at 1280×720, and consider a print stylesheet.
9. The **Gemini build** at `/gemini/` keeps its own look (don't restyle it); optionally make the link to it clearer.

## 7. How to deliver changes
- Propose a **short plan first** (what changes, which files, why). Andrew approves it before you write code.
- Then give changes as **complete replacement files** or clear **unified diffs**, one file at a time, small enough to review. Say which constraint each change might affect.
- Prefer CSS-only and markup changes over JS. In `ui.js` and `charts.js`, change presentation, not logic.
- No new dependencies or frameworks. Fonts: system stack or one Google Font at most.
- Andrew will apply the changes and run the tests (or have Claude Code do it), then report failures back to you.
